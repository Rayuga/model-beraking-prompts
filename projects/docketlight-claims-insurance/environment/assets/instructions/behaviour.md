# How claim work flows

The sample book carries claims at every stage, and the desk has to move one from intake through to settlement without losing a step. Two things have bitten us before and must not be possible again: an adjuster once set a reserve far above anything the policy could ever pay, and we once paid one claim twice because two settlement requests went through on the same file. Payable exposure is what the policy would really pay on a loss, capped by the policy limit, net of the deductible and never a negative figure; a reserve is a real positive sum set aside against that exposure and never exceeds it; a claim carries at most one payment, sized to the reserve a supervisor signed off; and releasing that payment settles the claim and leaves the single ledger debit behind.

Only the role that owns the step, inside its own region and its own assignments, from the state that allows it, may change a claim - a state move, an evidence item, a reserve request or approval, a settlement request or release, a reopen, or a draft correction. Wrong role, region, assignee, amount or state is turned away with nothing changed. Every write also carries the version of the claim the person was looking at, so a write that would change the claim and arrives from an older copy is turned away too, again with nothing changed. Settled files stay readable and stay closed.

What the desk accepts has to survive refresh, sign-in, and the application being stopped and started, without turning into two of anything. A settlement request carries a repeat-safe key that the desk remembers once for the whole book: the same key on the file it already paid gives back that one payment, and the same key anywhere else changes nothing. Evidence on file is photographs and estimates, and a claim cannot go for reserve review before a photograph is on file. A loss dated outside its policy's coverage window is refused at intake. While a draft is still open its creator may correct the reported loss, the payable exposure is worked out again, and the claim moves to submitted. A supervisor may reopen an open assigned claim and clear the assignment so another adjuster can take it, and a newer reserve request replaces the older one so only the newest pending reserve counts.
