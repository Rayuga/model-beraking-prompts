# Stored materials

Stored materials were the part that took the longest. Iron that sat in the
yard last month and stood up this month is installed work now. New deck at
the Downtown yard is a stored pile, not installed work, and it is not the
same county window as the job. Someone asked the warehouse tax office to
tax Harborview's installed steel because the deck skids live Downtown, and
the number that came back was the Downtown window on the wrong base.
Someone else taxed the new stored pile at Riverside. Both papers looked
fine on the grid and paid different cards. Converted iron is taxed where
the job lives, when it stands up. The pile itself is not a tax base for
the county, and fee, bond, retainage, fringe, and liquidated damages have
never been a tax base either. The warehouse window is a reference the yard
quotes for its own storage paperwork; it has never been a line the owner
pays on a draw. A draw carries one tax line and it is the job's county on
work in place. A till that added a warehouse line to the net billed the
owner for something nobody had agreed to pay.
