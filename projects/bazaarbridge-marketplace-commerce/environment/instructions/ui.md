# The desks we look at

We find our way by reading the screen, so every destination explains itself. The Administrator wants clear routes into Dashboard, Orders, Products, Inventory, Warehouse, Finance, Compliance and Admin; merchants want their own scoped Dashboard, Orders, Products, Inventory and Finance views; platform staff (including a Viewer) see the Admin desk read-only with people, roles and suspension state. The signed-in person, role and merchant scope are visible all the time, and it is obvious when an action is not theirs. Administrative writes belong to the Administrator alone and the server refuses them for every other role.

Orders search and filter by status across the visible collection. Products show price, stock, reserved and low-stock state. Warehouse shows shipments, receipts and return intake. Finance shows payouts with commission and processing broken out, refunds, and the working under any total. Compliance shows the audit trail and freeze state. Admin shows people, roles and suspension state.

We use light and dark themes with a visible switch that preserves where we are. At phone width the content stays usable without horizontal overflow and every workspace stays reachable. On desktop there is a navigation landmark, a main landmark, meaningful headings and accessible control names. Refusals stay visible with a useful reason until we move on; controls show clear hover or focus feedback; opening or closing a detail surface leaves the workspace coherent.

The screens should also read as one coherent design: type is clear and legible, colours keep their contrast in both themes, spacing and alignment look deliberate, and the important parts are easy to scan first — the same visual quality holds on a phone and a desktop.
