# How we talk in a matter

## Replies

Our clients ask several things at once, so answers have to stay with their question. You can reply to any message, and to a reply, at least three levels deep. Replies sit under the message they answer, in the order they were sent, even when other messages arrive in the matter in between. Each reply shows a short quote of what it is answering, so you can follow it without scrolling. A reply always belongs to the matter of the message it answers; nobody can reply from one matter to a message in another.

## Edits

People fix typos and figures, so authors can edit their own messages. An edited message says "edited", and our staff can open its earlier versions. Authors can also delete their own messages, unless a partner has put them on hold. Deleting can't be undone, so the app asks us to confirm first, and saying no leaves the message as it was. A deleted message with replies leaves the same placeholder as one a timer removed. Two of us sometimes have the same matter open; if someone saves an edit from a page that is out of date, because the message changed since they opened it, refuse the edit and keep what they typed so they can look again.

## Formatting

Clients paste all sorts into chat, and some of it comes from documents with odd characters or markup in them. We want a little formatting: `*bold*`, `` `code` ``, a line starting with `> ` shown as a quote, and any http or https address typed in a message shown as a link that opens in a new tab. Anything else that looks like markup, scripts or HTML is just text and shows exactly as it was typed, everywhere a message appears: in the thread, in a reply's quote, in search snippets, in earlier versions and in transcripts. Typing the words "On hold" into a message mustn't make it look held.

## Links to messages

Every message has a link we can copy and paste into another matter, so we can point at something said elsewhere. Pasted into a message, it shows a short preview of the linked message for anyone who can see that matter, and "not available" for anyone who can't or once the message is gone. Opening a message from a link or a search result takes you to it in its thread and highlights it, so you can spot it at once.

## Mentions

Typing `@` and a name (a first name is enough, in any letter case) mentions someone who can see the matter. They see it on their own list of mentions. As soon as you type `@` in the message box, the app suggests the people who can see that matter, and only them, and picking one puts their name in the message. Nobody can be mentioned in a matter they can't see: the message still posts, but a walled person never hears about it. Mentions follow what a message says now: editing a mention into a message puts it on that person's list, and editing it out or deleting the message takes it off again.

## Unread messages

Each of us has our own unread count per matter, and it is the same in every tab we have open. When I open a matter, it shows a "new messages" line where I stopped reading, and opening it marks it read up to the newest message. I can mark it unread again from any message, which moves the line there. Deleted messages don't count as unread, and a walled matter shows nothing at all.

## Sending and live updates

Enter sends a message and Shift+Enter starts a new line in it. Escape closes an open edit or reply without saving anything, leaving the message as it was.

Our internet in court is patchy. If the same message is sent twice because the first reply got lost, it must appear only once. Messages sent at the same time stay in the order they were sent wherever you see them: the thread, search and the transcript.

When someone else posts, replies, edits or a message disappears, every open tab should show it within about five seconds without reloading. That must not disturb what I'm doing: a half-typed reply, where I've scrolled to and an open thread all stay as they are.

The app should work on a phone at about 390 px wide without the page scrolling sideways.
