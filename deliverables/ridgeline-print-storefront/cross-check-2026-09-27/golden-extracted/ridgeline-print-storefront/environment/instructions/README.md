# The Ridgeline catalogue

You'll find eight prints and thirteen offered sizes in /assets/seed_data.json, along with our postage bands, sheet weights and an older order. The photographs in /assets/prints belong to those prints; please use the matching images. The price figures are in pence.

We keep stock separately for each print and size. That's what a variant means here: one SKU and one paper size. Please keep the supplied titles, papers, prices, stock counts and trade offers. When an A2 edition sells out, someone can still buy its A3 edition if copies remain. Sold-out sizes stay visible with their price and zero available, but can't be added to an order. Only call the whole print sold out when none of its sizes is available.

On the grid, use the lowest regular price across that print's offered sizes, even if the cheapest size has sold out. The size and stock-sheet filters also include sold-out editions; people still want to see them. Title searches shouldn't care about capitals. Sorting by price uses that same grid price, and sorting by title is alphabetical, with both directions available.

Opening a print should show a larger version of its real photograph and all its offered sizes. For each size, customers need the paper, regular price, available quantity and trade offer. There's no need to invent sizes that aren't in the catalogue.

## Trade prices

The trade offer belongs to a particular print and size. Once someone buys enough of that variant, every sheet on that line gets its trade price. Five sheets spread over different prints or sizes don't qualify an edition that needs five of its own. If someone adds the same variant again, combine the quantities. Dropping below the trade quantity brings back the regular price.

People should see the offer before they qualify and the saving once they do. In the basket and checkout, start with the full-price subtotal, then show the trade saving, postage and amount payable separately. The subtotal always uses regular prices. Changing the basket means working those amounts out again from its current lines.

## Postage

Postage depends on the combined weight of the sheets: 90 g for each A3 and 160 g for each A2. Letter is 175 pence up to 100 g, Large letter is 320 pence up to 500 g, and Small parcel is 495 pence up to 2,000 g. Those limits are inclusive. Use the first band that fits the weight.

Anything heavier is collection from the studio only, with no postage charge. Make that clear before someone orders. A trade offer changes the price, of course, but the sheets still weigh the same.
